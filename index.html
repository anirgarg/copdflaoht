Quick diagnostic flow

1) When to suspect COPD
  • Chronic and progressive dyspnea
  • Recurrent wheeze
  • Chronic cough
  • Recurrent respiratory infections
  • Risk factors including exposure to tobacco smoke
2) Confirm with spirometry

Diagnosis requires spirometry demonstrating post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.7 (or LLN).

Spirometry sites Next: reversibility check
3) Is airflow obstruction reversible with bronchodilators?

If FEV1 improves by ≥200 mL AND ≥12%, diagnosis is more likely to be asthma.

Yes → asthma more likely No → COPD confirmed
If reversible

Diagnosis is more likely to be asthma. Consider asthma management and reassess if mixed disease suspected.

COPD is confirmed → determine severity

Severity (spirometry) & referral guidance

Mild

FEV1 ≥80% predicted

Consider referring to a respirologist if not responding to treatment as outlined in the pathway.

Moderate

FEV1 50–80% predicted

Consider referring to a respirologist if symptomatic or FEV1 <70%. Document GOC discussion.

Severe / Very severe

Severe: FEV1 30–49% predicted
Very severe: FEV1 <30%

Patient should be seen by a respirologist. Document GOC discussion.

Assessing symptoms: consider COPD Assessment Test (CAT) and MRC grade.
Referral shortcut: Pulmonary Function Laboratory at KHSC – HDH site (see Resources).
