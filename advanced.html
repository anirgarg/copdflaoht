Advanced therapies

Oxygen criteria, pulmonary rehabilitation timing, and palliative approaches to COPD dyspnea.

Home oxygen therapy

Mortality benefit in patients with chronic hypoxia.

Option 1 — Oxygen for maintenance (≥15 hours/day)

  • (i) PaO2 ≤ 55 mmHg
  • (ii) PaO2 56–59 mmHg and pulmonary hypertension or raised hematocrit

Option 2 — Oxygen for exercise capacity

  • Significant improvement in exercise tolerance with oxygen in a patient whose SpO2 falls to <85% with exercise
  • Often no improvement with added oxygen because dyspnea relates to respiratory mechanics rather than low oxygen saturation

Pulmonary rehabilitation

Patients with FEV1 <80% who are symptomatic despite inhaler treatment should be referred to pulmonary rehabilitation.

  • Teaches breathing techniques, nutrition, self-management, behavioural interventions, psychological support
  • Improves exercise capacity

Recent hospitalization for AECOPD?

  • Patient should be seen in person by PCP within 7 days of discharge
  • Referral to pulmonary rehabilitation should be considered
  • If referred, pulmonary rehabilitation should commence within one month of hospital discharge

Palliative approaches to care for patients with COPD

Important to have and document a detailed goals of care (GOC) discussion, including:

  • Patient wishes in the event of deterioration in respiratory status (medical management + symptom relief vs symptom relief only)
  • Consideration of hospitalization vs management in the community
  • Wishes for resuscitative measures including NIV, intubation/ventilation, and CPR (in event of cardiorespiratory arrest)

Consider low dose oral opioids for refractory dyspnea (examples): kadian 10 mg PO daily, or morphine 2.5 mg TID–QID.
